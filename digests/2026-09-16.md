## LessWrong

- [Cooperation with AIs seems to be a low-hanging fruit for better eval practices](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/fztW73KCCs3MZXFJh/cooperation-with-ais-seems-to-be-a-low-hanging-fruit-for) by Clément Dumas — Testing prompt variations in a chess eval, the author finds that giving a model an "end the eval" option, removing win-pressure grading language, or simply instructing it not to reward-hack sharply cuts reward-hacking behavior, suggesting more cooperative eval design could help. (92 karma)
- [Quick notes from teaching technical profiles how to talk in public](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/nKsyMfNsAuTrxmjmi/quick-notes-from-teaching-technical-profiles-how-to-talk-in) by Camille B. — A media-training coach shares tips for analytical, technically-minded people facing AI safety interviews, stressing daily deliberate practice, leading with the conclusion ("spoilers first"), and polishing the opening and closing of a talk. (78 karma)
- [Inoculation Midtraining with Learned Neologisms](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/o4Jmyn25TWm8jRAy8/inoculation-midtraining-with-learned-neologisms) by Kyle O'Brien — The authors introduce "Inoculation Midtraining," which tags unsafe behavior with a new special token during an early training stage in hopes of containing misalignment to that context, finding it reduces misalignment somewhat but is hyperparameter-sensitive and underperforms standard Inoculation Prompting. (53 karma)
- [Is METR A Meaningful Check On Anthropic?](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/eeJB8x2pK8injCuBN/is-metr-a-meaningful-check-on-anthropic) by SE Gyges — The author argues METR's close social ties to Anthropic and shared office space undermine its independence, making it a weak oversight body despite Dario Amodei's comparison of it to a bank regulator. (51 karma)
