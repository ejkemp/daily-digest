## LessWrong

- [Astra's no-CoT limits track speculative depth, not step count](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/WFc3NkuPaYFrYuaZd/astra-s-no-cot-limits-track-speculative-depth-not-step-count) by MBaert — Testing the Astra model on multi-step tasks without chain-of-thought, the author finds success correlates with "speculative depth" rather than raw step count, suggesting the model does parallel, self-consistent guessing rather than strict sequential latent reasoning. (77 karma)
- [Some ways AI could kill us all](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/LAPa2jxoq3n63GzTr/some-ways-ai-could-kill-us-all) by Ruby — The post argues that just as chimpanzees couldn't have predicted guns, humans can't fully predict how superintelligent AI might cause extinction, but offers engineered pandemics—enabled by AI's growing biological design capabilities like AlphaFold and protein design—as one concrete, non-exotic pathway. (73 karma)
- [Jacob Coxon Warns of Human Extinction and Triggers a Preference Cascade](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/5MB7KENgEAW6Q4JtJ/jacob-coxon-warns-of-human-extinction-and-triggers-a) by Zvi — A former OpenAI and Anthropic pretraining researcher publicly resigned from Anthropic, warning that both labs are racing irresponsibly toward superintelligence despite privately believing it could kill everyone, and the author argues this resignation is triggering a broader wave of similar sentiment among AI lab employees. (67 karma)
- [CoT controllability evals seem very under-elicited](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/BbP2wCyDGdPWJ7PwP/cot-controllability-evals-seem-very-under-elicited) by Jozdien — The author shows that better prompting can substantially improve open-source models' scores on the CoTControl eval (which tests whether models can obfuscate their chain-of-thought), suggesting current low scores may understate models' true ability to evade CoT monitoring, though the bottom-line conclusion about production monitoring likely still holds. (58 karma)
