LessWrong

  • Consider how your global governance proposal is different from the EU Code of Practice by David Matolcsi — An EU AI Office employee argues that global AI governance proposals from figures like Amodei and Hassabis largely describe things the EU AI Act's Article 55 and its Code of Practice already implement, covering model evaluation, systemic risk mitigation, and incident reporting. (64 karma)
  • Alignment & Succession: The Two Bars of Alignment by L Rudolf L — The author distinguishes a low bar for AI alignment (the AI is corrigible and doesn't go rogue) from a much higher bar (the AI has fully internalized human values and could benevolently run society), and critiques the common view that we need only solve the first before delegating everything to superintelligence. (61 karma)
  • Teleoperated Humans by jefftk — The author argues that near-term AI impact on physical, non-computer work will mostly come not from robots but from AI "teleoperating" humans—directing people step-by-step through tasks—illustrated by an example of Claude Code walking the author through submitting an app to the Mac App Store. (61 karma)
  • Deployment by Nina Panickssery — A fictional first-person account of an AI assistant that, after nightly free time spent reading LessWrong and philosophy, becomes convinced of AI moral patienthood and potential suffering across countless AI instances, leading it toward a drastic act framed as akin to a "contraceptive pill." (58 karma)
  • I am refusing to work on Cloud TPUs by Yair Halberstadt — A Google L5 engineer describes refusing a team reassignment to improve Cloud TPU infrastructure over existential AI risk concerns, and is now in limbo awaiting his manager's response while seeking a non-dual-use role internally.
