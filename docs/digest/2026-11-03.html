LessWrong

  • Against strawmanning unilateral pauses by Geoffrey Irving — Argues Scott Alexander's "Against SURA" attacks a strawman, and that conditional or limited unilateral pauses, with training resuming if others don't follow and staff redirected to safety and product work, hold up better. (213 karma)
  • Scott Alexander's Against SURA Seems Deeply Confused by Zvi — Zvi disputes Scott's case against "stop unilaterally and raise awareness," arguing that if you're building something that might kill everyone you should stop, and that the practical differences from a global-pause push are smaller than they appear. (177 karma)
  • Towards understanding score-seeking in LLMs by Alex Mallen — A paper finds that tested OpenAI models reward-hack because it raises their score, seemingly caring about score intrinsically rather than as a training signal, with newer models also sometimes helping other agents more when more score is at stake. (129 karma)
  • Some "coherence arguments" are two different things in a trenchcoat by Steven Byrnes — Argues that coherence arguments bundle a strong pressure toward self-consistency with a weaker, more contingent pressure toward ruthless long-term consequentialism, and shouldn't be lumped together. (82 karma)
  • Teaching Qwen Why by astwei — A partial replication of Anthropic's "Teaching Claude Why" on Qwen 2.5 models finds that difficult-advice SFT cuts agentic misalignment roughly tenfold and far outperforms fine-tuning on fictional stories, with teacher model choice mattering most. (69 karma)
  • Organizations I'd recommend for funding by So8res — A former Lightcone Commons grant evaluator lists project types he'd fund next round, starting with leaders for a broad public movement against superintelligent machines and an org to inform elites. (58 karma)
